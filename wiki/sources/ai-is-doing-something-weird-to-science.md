# AI is doing something weird to Science

Source: [Mostly Harmless AI](https://blog.apiad.net/p/ai-is-doing-something-weird-to-science). Raw capture: [raw/ai-is-doing-something-weird-to-science.md](../../raw/ai-is-doing-something-weird-to-science.md).

## Summary

Alejandro Piad Morffis argues that the useful unit of analysis in [[ai-in-science]] is not the model or the human in isolation, but the loop connecting them. His frame is [[proposer-verifier-curator-loop]]: humans pose questions, models propose candidates, independent verifiers filter candidates, and humans curate what survives.

The article rejects both common extremes. The replacement narrative collapses all scientific roles into the model. The dismissal narrative treats the model as a trivial tool. Piad's middle position is that AI is a qualitatively new proposer: not a principal investigator, not a full scientist, but also not a hammer.

## Key Takeaways

- The article's slogan is that the loop does the discovery.
- AI's current scientific value is mostly in the proposer slot: fast, broad, creative candidate generation.
- The verifier is more important than the proposer because valid science depends on independent checks.
- Strong proposer plus weak verifier produces failure modes like Galactica: fluent, plausible, and ungrounded.
- Strong verifier plus weak proposer can still produce valid results, but more slowly.
- The author treats Claude's Cycles, Tao plus Lean, AlphaFold, and GNoME/A-Lab as different domains with the same loop shape.
- The loop is not new; older examples include Appel-Haken, Flyspeck, and AI Feynman.
- What changed after 2022 is that general-purpose LLMs can occupy the proposer slot across domains with natural-language specification.
- AI makes candidate generation cheaper, so paper count may decouple further from actual discovery.
- The scarce skill shifts toward question selection and verifier design.
- The practical diagnostic for AI-in-science claims is: what was the verifier, and who built it?
