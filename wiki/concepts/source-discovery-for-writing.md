# Source Discovery For Writing

Source discovery for writing is the practice of finding people, documents, communities, and records that can produce original material for an article or essay. [[how-to-write-stuff-no-one-else-can]] emphasizes people-based source discovery as a way to write pieces competitors cannot easily reproduce.

## Workflow From The Source

1. Choose a company, project, or event.
2. Use public interviews and coverage to build a timeline of major milestones.
3. Search LinkedIn for employees who joined, left, or changed roles around those milestones.
4. Record each person's name, title, department, and dates.
5. Look for overlooked but strategically placed people.
6. Search those names across Google, podcasts, and other public media.
7. Interview or consult with selected sources.
8. Ask each source who else should be contacted.

## Why Manual Work Matters

Brooks argues for manual mapping because it develops context. Raw LinkedIn titles and dates can mislead when read without company stage, timing, or surrounding evidence. The manual pass helps distinguish an apparent executive signal from a loose early-startup role or temporary title inflation. [[how-to-write-stuff-no-one-else-can]]
