# AI In Science

AI in science is the use of AI systems to help generate hypotheses, propose candidate structures or proofs, search spaces, write code, analyze data, or otherwise participate in scientific discovery workflows.

The [[ai-is-doing-something-weird-to-science]] source argues that AI's scientific role is best understood as part of a [[proposer-verifier-curator-loop]]. The model proposes candidates, but scientific validity comes from independent verification and human question selection.

## Working Frame

- Poser: chooses the question worth asking.
- Proposer: generates candidate answers, patterns, proofs, structures, or experiments.
- Verifier: independently checks whether a candidate is valid.
- Curator: decides which verified outputs matter and should be pursued or published.

## Main Risk

AI makes candidate generation cheaper. If the verification layer does not improve, science may produce more plausible papers without producing proportionally more reliable discoveries. [[ai-is-doing-something-weird-to-science]]
