# Sparks of Weight-Space Programming: LLMs can natively read & write neural network weights

Source URL: https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/
Authors: Rikiya Takehi, Jayden Teoh (co-first), Alex Zhang, Omar Khattab — MIT CSAIL
Published: 2026-11-02 (a "Response to Comments" section was added 2026-11-12)
Captured: 2026-11-16

## Main Points

- Thesis: frontier LLMs can read and write neural network weights roughly the way they read and write code, either from the raw weights alone or with tools such as a Python kernel. The authors call this **Weight-Space Programming**: treating weights as an editable program instead of leaving them entirely to an optimizer.
- Motivation: LLMs already optimize text artifacts (prompts, harnesses, training code), partly because there was no principled way to hill-climb on weights by reasoning. The authors argue reasoning-driven optimization now reaches differentiable networks directly.
- Three capabilities: **reading** (infer behavior or training data from weights), **fixing** (diagnose and repair failures by editing weights), **writing** (produce working weights from a natural-language spec).
- The model used throughout is GPT-6 Astra. Harnesses varied by task: no tools, a Python sandbox, attention-only tools, or a limited number of forward passes. Gradients and training loops were forbidden in most experiments.
- The authors explicitly say this is **not a method** — it is a concept and an early, emerging capability, limited by context length and harness design. They have not compared Astra with human interpretability researchers.

## Experiments (as reported)

1. **Identify the game from PPO policy weights** — four policies (grid Breakout, Pong, Pac-Man, Freeway), 17,253 weights each. Correct for all four, no tools.
2. **Identify the shape a DDPM generates** — circle/square/triangle, 23,824 weights, no forward passes. Correct shapes, but drawn as outlines although the models generate filled shapes; read as evidence of structural reading rather than mental execution.
3. **Identify language and induction heads from middle layers only** — three 7.2M-parameter char-level transformers (Python, romaji, English), input/output layers removed, no execution. Correct language; found all copying heads.
4. **Fix a failing Kinetix car-on-ramp PPO policy** — 171,664-weight actor. Explained the local-minimum failure, edited 3.5% of weights in one try: 0% → 100% success over 1,024 episodes.
5. **Write MinAtar policies from scratch** — zero-initialized 600→32→32→6 nets, no training, no rollouts. Breakout 517.4 vs PPO 15.8 (100M steps) / 25.3 (500M steps). Seaquest and Asterix mixed versus 500M-step PPO. (PPO baselines not fully tuned.)
6. **Write a 9×9 Go policy** — 1.48M parameters, written in a Python sandbox. With 0 forward passes beat two smaller PPO nets in more than half the games; with 50 forward passes beat a PPO net trained on 20M games.
7. **Find the sycophantic Gemma 3 270M** — two fine-tuned copies, frozen embeddings. Failed from weights alone in four attempts; succeeded in all three pairs with access to attention patterns on texts it wrote.
8. **Improve Qwen3.5-0.8B at math** — traced reasoning loops to one direction in the MLPs of layers 15 and 19; edited 131,072 weights (0.016%). With 50 forward passes: MATH-500 7.9% → 48.1%, GSM8K 13.8% → 67.1%, IFEval 21.0% → 62.3%. Results depend on thinking mode and sampling settings.
- **Negative result:** Gemma 3 270M math barely moved (6.8% → ≤7.7%). Authors' reading: weight editing can remove an obstacle but not add a missing ability.

## Speculated applications

Auditing without evaluation (e.g. backdoor detection), cheaper initialization, zero-data capability steering and unlearning, recursive self-improvement, grafting circuits between architectures.

## Caveats noted

- "Native" means the LLM reasons about the weights itself, not that no tools were used.
- Several experiments allowed more than reading: attention outputs (7), forward passes (6, 8).
- Failure cases: Gemma sycophancy from weights alone, Gemma math, outline-vs-filled shapes.
