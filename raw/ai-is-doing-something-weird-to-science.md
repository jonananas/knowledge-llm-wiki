# AI is doing something weird to Science

Source URL: https://blog.apiad.net/p/ai-is-doing-something-weird-to-science
Author: Alejandro Piad Morffis
Publication: Mostly Harmless AI
Published: 2026-05-25
Captured: 2026-08-26

Alejandro Piad Morffis argues that the important question in AI-assisted science is not whether "AI did the science" or whether "AI is just autocomplete". The useful object of analysis is the loop: a human poses a question, a model proposes candidates, an independent verifier filters them, and a human curates what survives.

## Main Points

- The article rejects both the replacement narrative and the stochastic-parrot dismissal.
- The central claim is that discovery happens in the interaction loop, not inside the model alone or the human alone.
- The proposed loop has four roles: poser, proposer, verifier, and curator.
- In current AI-assisted science, the model usually occupies the proposer role.
- The poser and curator remain human, and the verifier should be independent of the model.
- The article uses four modern cases to show the pattern: Claude's Cycles, Tao and Lean, AlphaFold, and GNoME/A-Lab.
- Claude's Cycles: Claude proposed patterns, Stappers curated explorations, and Knuth verified and proved the surviving claim by hand.
- Tao and Lean: LLMs propose proof steps and Lean's type-checker verifies proof terms.
- AlphaFold: the model proposes structures; physical experimental methods verify whether the structures hold.
- GNoME and A-Lab: models proposed stable crystal structures; automated lab synthesis and physical measurement verified candidates.
- The article also traces older versions of the same loop through Appel and Haken's four-color theorem proof, Hales's Flyspeck project, and AI Feynman.
- What changed around 2022 is not the existence of the loop, but the proposer slot: general-purpose LLMs can now fill it across domains with natural-language direction.
- The strongest warning example is Galactica, framed as a capable proposer deployed without a reliable verifier.
- The author argues that strong verifiers matter more than strong proposers: weak proposers plus strong verifiers can still produce valid science, but strong proposers plus weak verifiers produce confident nonsense.
- AI may increase paper production without proportionally increasing discovery because candidate generation gets cheaper while institutional verification remains slow, weak, or gameable.
- The article's diagnostic question for AI-in-science claims is: what was the verifier, and who built it?
- The article concludes that science needs more investment in verifier design, question selection, and human accountability, not just better proposers.

## Strategic Framing

The article positions AI as a new kind of lab member: indispensable and surprising in the proposer slot, but not a principal investigator, not a full scientist, and not accountable as an author. The scarce skill shifts toward posing worthwhile questions and building reliable verifiers.
